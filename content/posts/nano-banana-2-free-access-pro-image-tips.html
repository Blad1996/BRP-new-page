What is Nano Banana 2 and why it matters

Nano Banana 2 is Google’s new image model optimized for high-fidelity image generation and manipulation. Hands-on use cases and examples

Nano Banana 2 is versatile. Below are detailed, practical use cases that demonstrate its strengths and how to integrate it into real workflows.

1. Consistency across stages — the aging banana

Request: A symmetrical, ultra-realistic studio photograph showing the same banana aging across five stages (day 0, day 5, day 10, day 30, day 60) in natural lighting on a white table.

Why it’s useful: This showcases the model’s ability to keep the object identity consistent while applying controlled variations. It’s ideal for:

  • Product lifecycle visuals
  • Before-and-after storytelling
  • Scientific illustration or time-lapse style shots

Tips: Provide a detailed prompt, specify camera and lighting, and set the number of variations to match the sequence you want. Adding a reference photo of the original banana helps the model lock onto the exact object features.

2. Change the camera angle while preserving context

Request: Take a character photo and change the camera angle to a bird’s eye view while keeping lighting, room layout, and other scene elements intact.

Why it’s useful: It creates a 3D-like edit from a single image, enabling multiple angles for the same scene without a photoshoot. Use cases include:

  • Generating different framing options for thumbnails and B-roll
  • Producing novel angles for product showcases
  • Rapid iteration on compositional layouts

Tips: Use an uploaded reference image to anchor the transformation. Specify angle, distance, and whether to preserve reflections and shadows.

3. Billboard and text-heavy visuals without gibberish

Request: Generate a high-resolution photo of a large roadside billboard on a busy American highway during golden hour with the headline exactly reading stop scrolling and drive.

Why it’s useful: Nano Banana 2 excels at embedding clear, readable text into images — a major advantage for marketing creatives, ad designers, and social creators who need on-image typography to be accurate.

Tips: Provide the exact phrase in the prompt and specify typography style if needed, e.g., bold black text on a white background. Set the model to create multiple variations to pick the best composition and angle.

4. Image edits powering video narrative — color to black-and-white transition

Workflow: Turn an image from color to black and white, then use a video frames model (like VO3.1 available in the same platform) to animate the character transitioning from color to black and white while displaying a reaction.

Why it’s useful: This demonstrates the power of combining image generation with video models in one workflow. It’s perfect for short social stories, skits, or dramatic effects in ads without complex post-production.

Tips: Start by creating the edited image(s) with Nano Banana 2. Then import those frames into a video model and describe the action and emotions to animate. Short social clips (3 to 8 seconds) often perform best on platforms like TikTok and Instagram Reels.

5. End-to-end content creation — thumbnail and video at once (Corvette example)

Workflow: Generate an AI video of a white C8 Corvette driving down a California-like highway using a video model. Simultaneously, create a cohesive thumbnail in Nano Banana 2 that matches the visual style of the video.

Why it’s useful: Creating both assets inside the same ecosystem speeds up production, ensures visual consistency, and saves iteration time. This approach is ideal for creators who want to produce social media-ready video plus promotional images quickly.

Tips: Keep prompts consistent across models. Use the same mood keywords (golden hour, coastal highway, cinematic POV) for the video and thumbnail so they look like part of the same campaign.

Prompting and workflow best practices

To get the best results from Nano Banana 2, follow these practical tips:

  • Be precise — Include camera angle, lighting, time of day, and exact text strings when relevant.
  • Use reference images — Upload a few reference photos if you want the AI to match a specific object or person across variations.
  • Specify output settings — Choose resolution, style fidelity, and number of variations before generating.
  • Iterate rapidly — Generate multiple variations and then refine with incremental prompts rather than trying to get a perfect image in one go.
  • Combine models — Use Nano Banana 2 for image creation and other models like VO3.1 for video frames within the same platform for end-to-end content production.
