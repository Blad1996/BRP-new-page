Why Most AI Videos Still Fall Apart

AI video can produce a great-looking moment, but that is not always the same as producing a usable video. A polished two-second clip is cool. A coherent story, an ad, or a cinematic sequence requires more.

The biggest problems tend to be the same:

  • Very short generations: Many tools limit creators to tiny clips, forcing them to generate and edit together a huge number of fragments.
  • Inconsistent subjects: A person, location, vehicle, or product can change shape, colour, logo, or identity from one shot to the next.
  • Static-looking visuals: Some AI generations feel stiff, with predictable movement and none of the visual energy needed to stop a scroll.

SeeDance 2.5 is designed around solving those exact limitations. Instead of treating AI video like a novelty, it gives you controls that make it useful for actual creative work.

A car example makes the point perfectly. Start with an image of a Ferrari Testarossa 849, then prompt the model to show that exact car driving through a desert, a city, and the Malibu coastline. The point is not just to create a cool driving sequence. It is to keep the vehicle consistent as the environment changes.

With the right reference image, the car can retain its intended colour and recognizable design across each setting. If you want even more control over changing angles, you can upload multiple references of the same vehicle.

That opens the door to polished concept videos for products that may be hard, expensive, or impossible to film in multiple locations.

The same method works for a simpler ecommerce example: a bucket hat. Upload the product image, then ask for multiple models to wear and showcase it. The output can feature different people while maintaining the look of the hat itself, ending with a clear product-focused visual.

That is exactly the kind of creative that can be adapted into a UGC product ad. The model can vary the talent and scenes while the key branded object remains the centre of attention.
