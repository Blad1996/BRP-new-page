What Is Runable AI?

Runable is an AI agent platform designed to handle larger, multi-step work. Instead of opening one tool to write content, another to make graphics, another to build slides, another to code a page, and another to automate recurring tasks, you can work from one chat-based workspace.

The biggest difference is that it does not immediately rush into generating something random. Before it builds, it asks questions to clarify the goal, format, audience, visual style, deliverables, and scope. That means there is less back and forth trying to repair an output that missed the point from the start.

You can also see the work being completed in real time. Rather than sitting in front of a loading screen and hoping the result comes out well, you can follow the planning and creation process step by step.

Once an asset is created, Refine Mode lets you request focused changes. You can point to a particular section, leave an edit request, and have the agent make that adjustment in place.

The Core Features That Make This AI Agent Different

Memory, personalization, and design systems

Runable includes memory through its personalization settings. This allows it to retain preferences and use information already established in previous work. If your business positioning, target audience, design preferences, or content style have already been provided, you do not necessarily need to start from zero every time.

You can also add a design system so generated websites, decks, and visual assets align more closely with your brand. There are settings for different model preferences, including image and video generation, plus options to create custom voices by recording one, generating one, or asking Runable to help create it.

Workflows and skills

Complex work gets easier when it follows a repeatable process. Runable includes preloaded workflows for tasks such as TikTok ads and cover letter generation, and there are hundreds of workflow options available.

You can also create your own skills. A skill can be created within Runable or uploaded, giving you a way to make the platform more useful for the exact tasks your business or team repeats.

Plugins for the tools you already use

Runable connects with more than 3,000 apps through plugins. That matters because useful automation is not just about creating a document. It is about getting information where it needs to go.

For example, you can connect tools such as Google Drive, Google Sheets, and Gmail to extend a research or outreach workflow. Instead of treating AI output as a dead-end document, you can use it as the start of an actual operating system for your work.

Scheduled tasks and delivery notifications

You can schedule work in plain English and have it run daily, weekly, or monthly. Results can be delivered through email or Slack, with additional notification options such as mobile, iMessage, Telegram, and Discord.

That means a one-time prompt can become a recurring process. Monthly content planning, weekly influencer research, recurring reports, and other repetitive work no longer need to begin with a blank page.

Run Meet and communication integrations

Run Meet can join Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams calls and turn them into notes, summaries, and action items. The meeting material can then be used as a starting point for a slide deck, research project, website, or other output.

There is also Runclaw, which lets you tag the agent in Telegram, Slack, Discord, Teams, or iMessage and have it run a task. That is a seriously useful idea because the AI can be available where work is already happening.

Ask Mode, Agent Mode, and Model Selection

Runable gives you different ways to work depending on the task.

  • Ask Mode: Best for quick questions and answers, plugins, and image-related tasks.
  • Agent Mode: Best for longer, more complex work that requires planning, research, generation, and multiple steps.
  • Plan Mode: Can be turned on or off when you want more control over the planning stage.

There is also a model selector with Auto, Ultra, Max, and Normal options. The available choice affects the credit usage, so it makes sense to use the right level for the work rather than always selecting the most expensive option.

From the prompt area, you can select plugins and skills, attach invoices, add design systems, import projects, or upload files and images. The platform is clearly built around the idea that AI should work with your actual materials instead of only responding to isolated text prompts.

Use Case #1: Create a 30-Day Marketing Plan and the Content to Go With It

One of the most practical use cases is content marketing. If you run multiple channels, software products, or client accounts, creating a full monthly content plan can become a huge time drain.

A prompt such as “Build me a 30-day marketing plan for Content Buddy AI and the content to go along with it” can trigger a planning process that asks the right questions first.

For this example, the selected channels were Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, and YouTube Shorts. The primary goal was free trial signups. The requested deliverable was a PDF report, along with short-form video hooks and scripts, captions, hashtags, Instagram and LinkedIn carousels, and posts for X.

The target audience was defined as small creators trying to grow on TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube Shorts. Their core desire was straightforward: spend less time creating content while earning more views and followers.

What the marketing plan included

After receiving the context, Runable generated a proposed scope before moving forward. It outlined a 12 to 15-page PDF report, the content library, channel roles, content cadence, and carousel creation process.

The completed report expanded to 26 pages and included:

  • An executive summary and key findings
  • Funnel math and targets
  • Positioning and messaging pillars
  • Channel-specific roles
  • A 30-day content calendar
  • Carousel concepts and images
  • Content recommendations based on a four-posts-per-day cadence

The workflow even made clear that paid ads were outside the scope because the selected focus was organic content channels. That type of visibility is important. You can see the assumptions before investing time or credits in creating the final assets.

One generated carousel concept focused on reasons Reels receive no views. The broader point is that the agent can move from strategy to actual social content creation inside the same workspace.

In the past, this could require a marketing manager, social media manager, designer, and a lot of internal coordination. Here, the plan, written assets, and visual content can all begin from one well-defined prompt. If the process works for your business, it can also be scheduled to run every month.

Use Case #2: Find Influencers and Build an Editable Pitch Deck

Influencer research is another task that sounds simple until you actually need to do it properly. You have to identify relevant creators, check their platforms and audience, assess fit, consider budget, build a shortlist, and package everything in a document someone can use to make a decision.

Runable can handle that workflow with a prompt requesting 10 to 20 potential influencers, research-grade outputs, charts, citations, structured analysis, and a pitch deck.

For the example, the task was scheduled to run every Sunday at 9 a.m. The requested deck format was an editable PowerPoint. The platforms selected were YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and X. The creator tier was micro-creators with audiences between 50,000 and 250,000, with a rough monthly influencer budget of $10,000.

Why the approval step matters

Before building the entire deck, Runable created a slide for approval. This is one of my favorite parts of the process. You can review the visual direction, approve it, and then have the rest of the deck produced.

That is much better than letting an agent burn through time and credits creating a full presentation in a style you do not even like.

The completed influencer partner shortlist included the budget, creator count, recommended allocation, methodology, rationale, and creators reviewed. It ranked creators by fit and surfaced platform, follower information, and estimated rate details.

The deck could then be edited directly, exported as a PowerPoint or PDF, or opened in Google Drive and Google Slides. A simple change request, such as changing a name in the deck, could be submitted and applied automatically.

You could take this even further with plugins. After approving a shortlist, Google Sheets or Gmail integrations could support an outreach process. I would still treat outreach as a two-step workflow: research and approval first, then communication. That gives you control over who is actually contacted.

Use Case #3: Build a Landing Page With Payments and a Database

This is probably the craziest use case. A prompt can generate a complete landing page for a new offer, including sign-up functionality, payment collection, a database, and an admin dashboard.

In the example, the offer was AI automation support: five automations built each month for $297 per month. The landing page was intended for people who want to save time and make more money with AI.

Runable asked key setup questions, including:

  • The business or offer name
  • Whether payments should be recurring or one-time
  • Whether customer accounts and logins were needed
  • Which landing page sections should be included
  • The desired visual style
  • Whether to connect an existing Stripe account

The selected direction was a recurring monthly subscription, checkout for customers, an admin dashboard for the business owner, and a clean, light editorial style with plenty of white space.

From prompt to a functional website

Once the details were confirmed, the agent mapped out the landing page, sign-up and payment flow, database structure, and admin dashboard. It also identified the information needed later for payment setup, including a Stripe secret key, webhook secret, and admin password.

The result was a polished landing page built around the promise: five automations a month, built for you, not by you.

The website dashboard includes areas for analytics, SEO, domains, payments, and secrets. You can publish the project, change the domain, add a custom domain, adjust visibility settings, connect GitHub, open the project in VS Code, or download the code.

That is the big deal here. You are not limited to a static mockup. The goal is a full-stack website that can accept payments, use a database, be deployed live, and remain editable without needing to code every change manually.

Why All-in-One AI Agents Matter

Most people do not need another tool that can generate a paragraph. They need help moving an entire project from idea to execution.

A useful AI agent should reduce the number of handoffs required to get work done. It should help turn a rough idea into a strategy, assets, workflows, revisions, and recurring automation.

Runable is aiming directly at that problem. It combines content generation, design, research, presentations, app creation, websites, scheduling, integrations, meeting notes, and editing into one system.

Does that mean you should blindly automate everything? Of course not. The smartest approach is to use the agent for the heavy lifting, review important outputs, approve brand-facing work, and keep human judgment where it matters most.

But if you are currently paying separately for developers, designers, researchers, marketers, and disconnected software tools for every small project, this kind of workflow can completely change how quickly you operate.
