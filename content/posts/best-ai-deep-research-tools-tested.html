🔍 The Ultimate AI Research Showdown: Tools Compared

Choosing the right AI tool for deep research can be overwhelming, especially with so many options on the market. To make this comparison fair and insightful, I used the exact same research prompt across all platforms:

“Please craft a research report about how global warming is affecting certain animal populations in the United States. Please include plenty of graphs and photos that support what you find.”

This prompt was designed to test the tool's ability to conduct thorough research, compile visual data, and present findings clearly. Here’s how I conducted the test:

  • Submitted the prompt to Manus, Genspark, Grok, Deep Agent, ChatGPT (GPT-3.5 with deep research enabled), Gemini, and Perplexity.
  • Noted if the tools asked clarifying questions before starting research.
  • Examined the number of sources used, the inclusion of graphs and photos, and the overall quality of the research report.
  • Compared the length, depth, and visual appeal of the final outputs.

Clarifying Questions: Why They Matter

One key difference I noticed immediately was how some tools handled the initial prompt. Gemini, ChatGPT, and Deep Agent all asked clarifying questions to better understand the scope and specifics of the research before diving in. This is crucial because deep research often requires context—such as preferred data types, focus areas, or report length—to deliver the most relevant and useful output.

On the other hand, Grok, Genspark, Manus, and Perplexity jumped straight into the task without any further input. While this approach might save time, it risks producing results that don’t fully align with user expectations.

📊 Detailed Tool Analysis: How Each AI Performed

Gemini: Ambitious but Ultimately Disappointing

Gemini started strong by analyzing 169 different websites, which seemed promising. However, after all that effort, it admitted difficulty fulfilling the request and asked if I wanted help with something else instead. This was surprising and disappointing, especially since many users have reported similar experiences where Gemini fails to complete deep research tasks despite extensive data gathering.

ChatGPT (GPT-3.5): Decent but Limited in Visuals

ChatGPT took about seven minutes, pulling from 33 sources and performing 90 searches. It produced a report with some graphs and tables, but these visuals were small and sparse. The report included one or two photos but lacked the rich, detailed charts I was hoping for. While it cited sources properly, the overall depth and presentation felt underwhelming compared to the expectations for a deep research tool.

Grok: Underwhelming Visuals and Limited Scope

Grok’s report was surprisingly minimal. Despite explicitly requesting numerous graphs and photos, it only produced a small table and no real visuals. The research itself was shallow, covering just four data points. For users expecting a comprehensive, visually rich report, Grok’s output felt incomplete and disappointing.

Genspark: Visual PowerPoint Style but Lacking Depth

Genspark created a presentation-style report with plenty of photos and graphs. However, it lacked detailed textual explanations, making it feel more like a slideshow than a research report. This format could be useful for visual learners or quick overviews but doesn’t fulfill the needs of users who want in-depth analysis and context alongside visuals.

Manus: Comprehensive but Missing Visuals

Manus impressed with a 44-page report full of detailed research. It clearly invested time and effort into gathering information but failed to include the requested graphs and photos. For academic or professional use where visuals support findings, this is a significant drawback. The lack of images means users might need to supplement the report manually.

Perplexity: Balanced but Short

Perplexity recently updated its research features and showed improvement. It included several photos and graphs, and referenced 48 sources. However, the report was relatively short and lacked the depth expected for a complex topic like climate change’s impact on wildlife. It’s a solid choice for quick overviews but may not satisfy users needing extensive details.

Deep Agent: The Clear Winner

Deep Agent stood out as the best performer. It produced a 23-page report complete with an executive summary, introduction, methodology section, and multiple detailed graphs and photos. The visuals were descriptive and relevant—for example, showing temperature changes and drought-affected areas in California. It even included priority conservation areas for endangered species, supported by clear, informative charts.

What impressed me most was the balance of depth, clarity, and visual support. Deep Agent not only answered the research question thoroughly but also presented the information in a professional, easy-to-digest format. It took minutes to generate what would have taken weeks for a human researcher.
