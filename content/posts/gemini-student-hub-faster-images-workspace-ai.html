Google Gemini has quietly added a stack of new features that make it far more useful for students, creators, teachers, and anyone trying to get more done in Google Workspace. From a dedicated Gemini Student Hub to faster image creation, AI built directly into Docs, Sheets, and Slides, plus smarter NotebookLM sharing, these updates unlock some seriously powerful new Gemini use cases.

The big shift is that Gemini is becoming less of a standalone chatbot and more of an assistant that can work with your class materials, documents, email, spreadsheets, presentations, and research. If you are still only using it for basic questions, you are leaving a lot on the table.

1. Google Gemini Student Hub Makes Studying More Personal

One of the most useful additions is the new Student Hub inside Google Gemini. It is designed to help students organize classwork, turn materials into study resources, and learn in a format that actually fits the way they study.

Once inside the Student Hub, you can add class materials and build a dedicated study notebook around a course. Instead of asking random questions in a fresh chat every time, Gemini can work from the content you provide, such as a syllabus, class notes, and other learning materials.

That gives you a more structured way to prepare for assignments, exams, and the full semester.

What You Can Create in Gemini Student Hub

  • Custom study notebooks for individual classes
  • Flashcards based on your course materials
  • Quizzes to test what you know
  • Custom lessons tailored to your needs
  • Podcasts, images, and infographics to support different learning styles

The process is interactive. When you ask Gemini to help create a study notebook, it can ask follow up questions to understand the class, your goals, and what kind of support you need. That is important because a generic study guide is not nearly as useful as one built around the actual material you are learning.

Google also includes a free student plan that can be claimed from this area. For students, this could be a major advantage. Someone who consistently uploads materials, quizzes themselves, and reviews flashcards is likely going to be much more prepared than someone trying to cram everything the night before.

This is not only useful for students. Teachers, tutors, and academics can upload a syllabus and class notes, then use Gemini to help structure learning resources for others. It creates a practical system for transforming raw teaching materials into organized study support.

2. Control Watermarks on Gemini Generated Media

Another quiet but significant update is a new media watermark setting. Inside Gemini’s settings menu, there is an option to turn media watermarking on or off.

When enabled, Gemini adds a watermark to media it creates, including images, videos, and music. The new setting gives you the ability to disable that watermark directly rather than generating the content and then trying to remove it in a separate tool.

That matters because exporting a file, uploading it elsewhere, editing it, and downloading it again can create extra friction and potentially affect quality. Having this option available within Gemini keeps the creation process simpler.

If you create content regularly, this is one of those small settings worth checking immediately. It can save time across dozens of image, video, or audio generation tasks.

3. Faster Gemini Image Generation With Flash 3.7

Gemini’s image creation experience is also getting faster through the use of Flash 3.7. Speed is a bigger deal than it sounds, especially when you are testing multiple creative directions, refining generated assets, or building images for social media, presentations, and professional branding.

The image area also includes a collection of templates. Options include formats such as headshots, bento-style visuals, Miro-style boards, slides, and sitcom-inspired imagery, with more templates available from the broader menu.

Templates help when you know the type of asset you need but do not want to start from a blank prompt. Still, the quality of the result depends heavily on how clearly you explain what you want.

A Better Prompt Produces a Better AI Headshot

A simple experiment makes the point perfectly. Start with an ordinary personal photo and use a basic request such as asking Gemini to turn it into a LinkedIn headshot. Gemini can generate a result quickly, but the output may not feel accurate. Hair can look wrong, facial features may shift, and the final image may not look professional enough to use.

The difference comes from prompt quality.

A stronger prompt gives Gemini more context about the situation, task, objective, and desired outcome. Rather than saying only “make this a LinkedIn headshot,” you can specify that you want a polished, professional-looking headshot that maintains your likeness and makes you look credible, approachable, and employable.

That extra direction can dramatically improve the output. The model has a much clearer target, and the result is more likely to look intentional rather than randomly AI generated.

Use a Prompt Optimizer for Better Results

One tool that can help is MyPromptBuddy, a prompt optimizer that can refine a simple idea into a more complete prompt. It is useful for image prompts, normal chat prompts, reasoning tasks, deep research, and reusable prompt shortcuts.

The goal is not to make prompting complicated. The goal is to provide enough useful detail that the AI understands what success looks like.

For image generation, include details such as:

  • Purpose: LinkedIn profile, website, slide deck, social post, or marketing asset
  • Subject: Who or what should be featured
  • Style: Professional, colourful, clean, editorial, cinematic, or minimal
  • Composition: Close-up, waist-up, landscape, square, or portrait orientation
  • Important constraints: Preserve facial likeness, use natural lighting, avoid distorted details, or match a brand feel

Good prompting is one of the highest leverage skills in AI. A better prompt can be the difference between something that looks unusable and an image you can confidently publish.

4. Gemini in Google Docs Can Write, Format, and Draft Emails

Some of the biggest Gemini updates are happening inside Google Drive. Open a new Google Doc and you can access Gemini tools that help with formatting, writing, meeting notes, email drafts, research, and more.

One especially practical feature is the ability to match another document’s format. If your organization has a preferred template, voice, or layout, this can save a huge amount of manual formatting work.

You can also use Gemini to match the writing style of a specific document. That is useful when you need a new document to sound consistent with an existing proposal, report, memo, or internal resource.

Turn Documents Into Gmail Drafts

Gemini can help structure email drafts directly within Docs. You can prepare the recipient, CC and BCC fields, subject line, and body content, then connect it to Gmail and convert the finished draft into an email draft.

That is a simple workflow improvement, but it removes a lot of repetitive copy and paste. If you routinely create detailed emails from meeting notes, reports, or client updates, building the content in Docs and sending it to Gmail can make the process smoother.

Use Your Connected Information as Context

Gemini can also be connected to sources such as Google Drive, Gmail, and web history to gather relevant context. This is where Gemini becomes much more than a writing assistant. Instead of manually searching through files and emails, you can use connected information to support the task you are working on.

It can also create images from within Docs, giving you another way to create visual assets without switching between tools.

5. Google Sheets Can Build Models, Dashboards, and Trackers

Gemini inside Google Sheets may be one of the most useful updates for business, finance, operations, and planning work. Sheets now offers AI-supported suggestions for budgets, forecasts, trackers, analysis, dashboards, data cleanup, rollover templates, and data audits.

Tasks that previously took a long time to build manually can now be created from a prompt and then refined to fit your needs.

Useful Gemini for Sheets Use Cases

  • Build a department budget
  • Create a pricing model
  • Set up a financial forecast
  • Generate a tracker from existing files
  • Create a reporting dashboard
  • Clean up and audit spreadsheet data
  • Research a topic and bring relevant information into a sheet

For example, Gemini can be asked to create a discounted cash flow model for a public company. It can conduct the research, bring the information together, and create the model inside Sheets.

That does not mean you should blindly trust every output. Financial models, source data, and calculations still need review. But the amount of setup work that Gemini can eliminate is massive. Instead of spending hours building a first draft of a spreadsheet, you can start with an AI-generated framework and focus on validation, assumptions, and decision-making.

6. Generate Research-Backed Presentations in Google Slides

Google Slides is now capable of generating presentations from a simple description. You can tell Gemini the subject, desired slide count, audience, visual style, level of detail, connected sources, and whether the deck should match the format of another presentation.

For example, you could request a five-slide presentation explaining how the TikTok algorithm works. Gemini can then ask clarifying questions before it begins.

Those questions can cover areas like:

  • Whether the deck should match an existing slide style
  • Which sources it should use
  • What connected apps or web sources are available
  • Who the presentation is for
  • Whether the design should be energetic, colourful, formal, or minimal
  • Whether the content should be concise or more detailed
  • Which specific topics should be included

After you approve the outline, Gemini can research the topic, generate the slide content, format the deck, and create the graphics. The slides remain editable, so you can adjust the design, rewrite content, enhance individual slides, or make changes to fit your voice.

This is a major workflow change. A presentation that once required researching, outlining, designing, formatting, and building from scratch can now begin with a researched, professional-looking first draft in minutes.

It is especially useful when you need a starting point for internal presentations, teaching materials, client ideas, content planning, or strategic concepts. You can also provide another slide deck, spreadsheet, meeting notes, or research materials as reference points.

7. NotebookLM Adds Private Notebook Copies and Better Analysis

NotebookLM has also received a helpful sharing update. You can now create a copy of a notebook, which makes a private version in another account.

The copied notebook includes the sources and studio content from the original. However, it does not include chat history or personal notes.

This is much better than relying only on standard sharing when you want someone else to have access to the useful material without giving them the ability to change your original notebook.

Why Private Notebook Copies Matter

Traditional sharing can get messy. Someone might remove a source, add unrelated notes, change materials, or otherwise clutter the notebook. That can make the original resource less useful over time.

Copying solves that problem. You can give someone a version of your notebook to explore and use while keeping your own source library, notes, and chat history protected.

This is ideal for educators sharing research notebooks with students, teams distributing internal knowledge resources, or creators offering structured learning materials without opening up their own workspace to edits.

NotebookLM has also become better at analyzing data and creating charts, thanks to improvements to the model powering it. If you are working with research-heavy material, this makes the tool more capable of turning source content into useful analysis and visual summaries.

The Bigger Picture: Gemini Is Becoming an AI Workspace

These Google Gemini updates are not random features. Together, they point toward a much bigger shift.

Gemini is becoming an AI layer across the tools people already use every day. It can support studying from class materials, refine prompts for better images, work with documents and emails, build spreadsheets, research topics, generate presentations, and package knowledge inside shareable notebooks.

The opportunity is not to hand over every task without thinking. The real advantage comes from using Gemini to eliminate repetitive setup work, create stronger first drafts, organize information, and give yourself more time for the parts that require judgment.

Start with one workflow. Build a study notebook. Create a dashboard in Sheets. Turn an outline into a slide deck. Use a better prompt for your next professional image. Once you see how much time it can save, it becomes hard to go back to doing everything manually.

Keep Exploring the Latest AI Tools

Google Gemini is moving quickly, and the most useful updates are often the ones that appear quietly inside the tools you already have open. Test these features against real work, keep control of the final output, and use AI as leverage rather than a replacement for your own judgment.
