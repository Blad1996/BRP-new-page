What Is Hedra MCP for Claude?

Hedra is not simply an image generator or a video generator. It is a research and production agent that can work across images, videos, and audio. It can also determine which available model is best suited to a particular creative task.

MCP, short for Model Context Protocol, is the connection layer that lets an AI platform use outside tools. Once Hedra’s MCP server is connected to Claude, Claude gains access to Hedra’s creative capabilities. That means Claude is no longer limited to writing instructions for content. It can initiate the production process itself.

The practical result is simple: you can describe what you want in plain language and have Claude coordinate the work.

For example, you can ask Claude to research what makes an effective thumbnail around AI voice agents for small businesses, develop the concept, and generate the actual thumbnail. Or you can ask for a short script about an AI news update, then have that script turned into a finished avatar video with voice and lip sync.

This is especially useful for creators, marketers, founders, agencies, and anyone who has plenty of ideas but wants to reduce the friction between planning and publishing.

How to Connect Hedra MCP to Claude

The setup is incredibly quick. You need a Hedra account and the Hedra MCP server URL, then you connect it through Claude’s connector settings.

  1. Go to Hedra and locate the MCP server connection URL.
  2. Copy the MCP URL.
  3. Open Claude and select Customize.
  4. Open the Connectors section.
  5. Select Add and enter Hedra as the connector name.
  6. Paste the Hedra MCP URL and continue.
  7. Complete the authentication process by signing in to your Hedra account.
  8. Allow Claude to access your account, then enable the available permissions and tools.

Once the connection is complete, Claude can access Hedra’s image, video, and audio capabilities from inside your chat. You can inspect the permissions and available tools before enabling them, which is important because those permissions are what allow Claude to actually carry out creative tasks instead of merely describing them.

Hedra MCP can also be used in other environments that support MCP connections, including tools such as Cursor and ChatGPT. The core idea remains the same: connect the production layer to the AI assistant you already use for research, writing, and planning.

Use Quality Levels Strategically

Image generation can involve a tradeoff between speed and quality. A high-quality output may take longer to render, while low or medium quality can be useful when you are testing concepts quickly.

  • Low quality: Useful for rapid idea exploration.
  • Medium quality: Useful when you want a stronger draft without waiting as long.
  • High quality: Best when you are ready for a final creative asset.

A smart workflow is to test several thumbnail directions quickly, decide which concept has the strongest hook, then create the final version at a higher quality setting.

Make Precise Edits Without Rebuilding Everything

The editing flow is where this gets even better. After generating a thumbnail, you can request one specific change without asking for a completely new design.

For example, if the image is right but the headline needs more contrast, tell Claude exactly what to modify: change only the text that says “AI answers calls” to bright yellow, while leaving everything else unchanged.

That level of specificity matters. It preserves the composition you liked while updating the one element that needs attention. Instead of endlessly regenerating new versions and hoping the model keeps the right details, you can make focused creative corrections.

Keep an Eye on Model Costs

Some video models may involve generation costs. Claude can surface a warning before proceeding, which gives you the chance to confirm the model choice. This is useful because video production is generally more resource-intensive than generating an image or audio clip.

Do not treat every model the same. If you need a polished final video, using a premium option may be worth it. If you are testing hooks, scripts, or avatar direction, start with a lower-cost approach where possible.

How to Prompt Claude and Hedra for Better Results

The better your direction, the better the finished creative tends to be. You do not need to write massive prompts, but you should provide a few important constraints.

For images and thumbnails, include:

  • The topic and intended audience
  • The core message or emotional hook
  • Required headline text
  • Important colours or brand cues
  • Any elements that must not change during revisions

For avatar videos, include:

  • Script length, such as 10 to 15 seconds
  • The message and intended platform
  • Whether to use an existing image, avatar, or voice
  • The preferred style, such as professional, energetic, or news-like
  • Your budget or quality preference if multiple models are available

For audio overviews, include:

  • The topic that needs research
  • The desired voice
  • The target duration
  • The intended audience
  • Whether the output should sound like news, an announcement, or a social update

Also, be specific when revising. “Make it better” is vague. “Keep the composition unchanged, but turn the headline bright yellow and check the spelling” is a useful instruction that produces a targeted result.
