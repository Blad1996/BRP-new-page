🚀 Why Build an AI-Powered Browser?

Arvind’s decision to build Perplexity’s own web browser, Comet, was driven by both offensive and defensive motivations. The origins of the idea trace back to understanding user behavior: the majority of search queries worldwide funnel through the browser’s URL bar, also known as the omnibox. This single entry point processes approximately fifteen billion Google queries daily, making it the prime battleground for search and AI integration.

Historically, Google’s dominance in search traffic was bolstered by strategic placements like the Google Toolbar, which significantly increased search volume and ad revenue. However, this dominance also exposed vulnerabilities. For example, Microsoft’s attempts to block the Google Toolbar on Internet Explorer led Google to develop Chrome — their own browser — to maintain control over the user experience and traffic.

Arvind draws parallels between Google’s historical browser strategy and Perplexity’s current situation, noting the challenges of building AI features on top of third-party browsers. Chrome updates have disrupted Perplexity’s Chrome extensions in the past, leading to inconsistent user experiences and limitations in agent capabilities. This underscored the need for full control over the client — a dedicated browser that could seamlessly integrate AI agents and deliver a smooth, secure experience.

The Comet browser is not just a defensive move to avoid reliance on others but an offensive strategy to unlock possibilities that cannot be achieved otherwise. By owning the browser, Perplexity can embed AI agents that deeply integrate with the browsing experience, enabling complex tasks such as:

  • Researching multiple tabs and compiling contextual information.
  • Interacting with user data in apps like Slack, Notion, and Google Docs to manage emails and calendars.
  • Assisting small business owners with pricing strategies and marketing by analyzing online discussions and reviews.

These tasks require a browser environment that can handle real-time context and user data securely and efficiently — something that a standalone chatbot or cloud-hosted agent cannot deliver effectively.

🧩 The Architecture of Comet: Hybrid Client-Server Model

A key differentiator of Comet is its hybrid architecture that combines client-side data privacy with the computational power of server-side AI models. Arvind emphasizes that user logins, passwords, and sensitive data remain on the client side, meaning Perplexity’s servers never store or access these credentials. When users prompt the AI to perform tasks, the browser agent only pulls relevant information from the tabs currently open on the client, ensuring privacy and security.

The heavy lifting of AI reasoning is performed by cloud-based models because current models are too large to run locally with acceptable performance. However, users have full control over their data, with options to delete queries or browse in incognito mode to avoid data retention entirely.

This approach contrasts strongly with other AI assistants that store authentication tokens and user sessions on their servers, raising security concerns. By keeping user data local and only interacting with the server for ephemeral AI computations, Comet strikes a balance between utility and privacy.

Additionally, the browser’s omnibox integration allows users to interact directly with AI agents wherever they are on the web, making the AI not just a separate tool but a co-browsing assistant embedded in the natural workflow of web navigation.

🔥 Competing Against Google and the Browser Wars

Given Google’s entrenched position as the dominant browser and search provider, competing against them is no small feat. Arvind is clear that Perplexity is not interested in playing for second place; their ambition is to become number one by building a browser and AI experience that surpasses Google’s offerings.

Google’s reluctance to fully integrate AI agents into Chrome at scale is driven by economics and risk management. If AI agents start autonomously clicking links, reading reviews, and making purchases, it threatens Google’s lucrative AdWords business model, which depends on human clicks and ad interactions. Google’s cautious rollout of AI features, such as Project Mariner locked behind high subscription fees, reflects their balancing act between innovation and protecting their core revenue streams.

Moreover, the operational challenges of serving billions of users with inference-heavy AI models make it difficult to deliver such features universally and reliably in the near term. This opens a window of opportunity for startups like Perplexity to innovate faster and capture early adopters with superior AI-driven browsing experiences.

Arvind also highlights the bureaucratic complexity within Google, where AI development, browser engineering, and ad sales teams must coordinate, slowing down feature deployment. Perplexity’s nimble structure allows for rapid iteration and focused development on AI-agent integration within the browser.

🤖 The Future of Agentic Web Browsing

What does the future look like when AI agents become an integral part of web browsing? Arvind envisions a world where the traditional human-internet interaction is mediated by AI agents that filter noise, summarize content, and automate mundane tasks.

With the proliferation of AI-generated content, the signal-to-noise ratio on the web is deteriorating. AI agents like those in Comet can help users by:

  • Filtering out spam, bots, and low-quality AI-generated content.
  • Summarizing relevant articles, social media posts, and professional connections.
  • Managing social networks by prioritizing meaningful connection requests and vetting contacts based on reputation criteria.

For example, event hosts can use these agents to sift through hundreds of requests, prioritize attendees based on predefined standards, and even automate communications. Such delegation saves users hours of tedious work.

Ultimately, Arvind hopes AI agents become personal digital assistants that tailor the online experience for each individual, providing curated, trustworthy information and automating routine workflows.

🧠 Choosing and Training AI Models for Perplexity

Perplexity’s approach to integrating AI models is methodical and user-focused. They maintain an internal benchmark called PPLXBench, a dynamic evaluation suite that grows with real user feedback and product needs. Bugs and issues reported from actual users are incorporated into this benchmark, guiding post-training and prompt engineering efforts.

This process ensures that model improvements are aligned with real-world use cases rather than just academic benchmarks. It also prevents model providers from overfitting to publicly known benchmarks since PPLXBench remains private and continuously updated with fresh user data.

Perplexity uses a mix of models for different tasks, not relying solely on a single chat model. For example:

  • Classifiers to detect when personal data or specific UI elements are needed.
  • Models specialized in generating charts, shopping cards, or handling sports queries.
  • A primary summarization and conversational model that orchestrates outputs and synthesizes information.

Training involves both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF), utilizing advanced algorithms like GRPO developed in collaboration with DeepSeq. This multi-model, multi-step approach allows Perplexity to deliver a sophisticated and context-aware AI experience.

🏋️‍♂️ Competing with Frontier Model Providers

Does Perplexity aim to compete head-to-head with AI giants who develop frontier large language models (LLMs)? Arvind’s answer is nuanced.

While Perplexity possesses the expertise to train state-of-the-art models, the real challenge lies in the ongoing investment and scale required to sustain leadership in model development. Training models is not a one-time event but a continuous race requiring massive GPU clusters, energy resources, and top talent.

Arvind sees the future of AI being won not just by the best models, but by those who excel in context engineering, tool orchestration, and delivering seamless user workflows. The model is a critical component, but the moat lies in integrating AI meaningfully into applications like browsers, where the AI can directly act on user intent and web content.

Perplexity is currently leveraging powerful models like DeepSeq’s offerings and eagerly anticipates models like Kimi, which show promise in internal evaluations. They also envision developing specialized, smaller models optimized for fast, local inference to control browser tabs and automate tasks efficiently. This would enable running AI locally on devices, preserving privacy and speeding up interactions.

The trend towards distillation and reinforcement learning techniques will make it possible to shrink state-of-the-art model capabilities into lightweight versions suitable for client-side deployment. Arvind is confident this shift will occur within the next year or so, revolutionizing AI interaction paradigms.

🔐 Privacy and Data Tracking in Comet

Privacy is a paramount concern for users interacting with AI-powered tools. Arvind clarifies misconceptions around data tracking and ad targeting with Comet.

While hypotheticals about AI-driven advertising were discussed in other contexts, Perplexity’s stance is clear: they are actively working toward a future without ads. Instead, they envision a subscription or usage-based model where users pay for the tangible value AI agents provide by completing tasks and saving time.

Perplexity does not collect logins, passwords, or sensitive credentials. Queries and context are processed temporarily, with users retaining the option to delete data or operate in incognito mode to avoid any data retention. This zero-retention policy ensures maximum user control over personal information.

Arvind contrasts this model favorably with other AI assistants that store authentication tokens and sessions on their servers, which poses a security risk. Comet’s architecture keeps all sensitive data local, using server-side AI only for ephemeral computation.

💼 AI, Jobs, and the Future of Work

One of the most pressing societal questions is how AI will impact employment, especially for white-collar jobs. Arvind provides a candid perspective on this.

AI will inevitably cause labor displacement, particularly in roles that involve repetitive cognitive tasks. Those who adapt quickly by mastering AI tools will become more employable, while others may struggle to keep pace with the rapid evolution of technology.

The challenge lies in the unprecedented speed of AI progress. Unlike past technological shifts, AI evolves every three to six months, requiring continuous learning and adaptation. Educational efforts alone are insufficient to prepare everyone for this fast-moving landscape.

Arvind stresses the importance of entrepreneurship and job creation by displaced workers themselves. As companies require fewer employees due to AI efficiency, new businesses and roles will emerge, often founded by those who lost traditional jobs but leveraged AI to create new opportunities.

He rejects both extreme dystopian and utopian views, instead offering a realistic outlook: AI integration will be disruptive but also transformative, demanding proactive engagement from individuals to remain relevant.

Arvind advises people to reduce unproductive activities like endless social media scrolling and invest time in learning AI usage. The future workforce will be those who can effectively harness AI to augment their productivity and creativity.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is Perplexity’s Comet browser, and why is it important?

Comet is Perplexity AI’s own AI-powered web browser designed to integrate AI agents deeply into the browsing experience. It offers users enhanced research capabilities, task automation, and privacy by keeping sensitive data on the client side. Comet aims to redefine web browsing by making AI a seamless co-pilot rather than a separate tool.

How does Comet ensure user privacy?

Comet keeps all authentication tokens, passwords, and personal data on the user’s device. AI computations requiring user data are temporary, with options to delete queries or browse in incognito mode. Perplexity does not store sensitive information on its servers, ensuring robust privacy protections.

Why is Perplexity building its own browser instead of relying on Chrome or Safari?

Building their own browser allows Perplexity to control the entire user experience, avoid disruptions from third-party browser updates, and embed AI agents that can interact with tabs, user data, and workflows in ways that extensions or cloud-hosted solutions cannot.

How does Perplexity compete with Google in AI and browsing?

Perplexity leverages faster innovation cycles, a hybrid client-server architecture for privacy and performance, and a focus on agentic AI that automates complex tasks. Google’s economic incentives and bureaucratic challenges slow its AI integration, giving startups like Perplexity an opportunity to innovate.

What is the future business model for Perplexity?

Instead of relying on ads, Perplexity envisions a subscription or usage-based model where users pay for AI agents that save time and increase productivity. They may also generate revenue from transaction commissions when agents make purchases on users’ behalf.

Will AI replace jobs?

AI will displace certain roles, especially those involving repetitive cognitive tasks. However, it will also create new opportunities for those who adapt by learning AI tools and entrepreneurship. The transition will be challenging due to the rapid pace of AI development.

Can AI models run locally on devices soon?

Arvind expects that within a year or so, advancements in model distillation and hardware will enable smaller, efficient AI models to run locally on devices, enhancing privacy and speed. This will be a game-changer for AI integration in browsers and applications.
