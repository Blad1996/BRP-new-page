Most vibe coding comparisons focus on the easy stuff: a landing page, a tiny dashboard, or a quick prototype that looks cool for five minutes. That is not the real test.

The real question is whether an AI app builder can handle an actual business process. Can it manage clients, estimates, projects, invoices, databases, permissions, and automations without turning into an expensive mess of prompts and workarounds?

To find the best vibe coding app for 2026, I put Softr, Lovable, and Base44 through the same build. The goal was to create a contractor estimate and project tracker that could realistically be used by a business, not just shown off in a demo.

The result was pretty clear: all three tools can generate something that looks like an app. But only one gave me a practical way to build, edit, operate, and automate a real business application from one place.

The Real-World Vibe Coding Test

For this comparison, each platform received the same prompt: build an all-in-one contractor estimate and project tracker.

The requested application needed to manage the complete process from lead to paid invoice. That meant it needed more than polished screens. It needed a working structure behind those screens.

What the contractor app needed to include

  • Lead and client management
  • An internal contractor dashboard
  • An estimate builder
  • Active project tracking
  • Invoice management
  • A project calendar
  • A client portal
  • Custom database tables for users, clients, estimates, projects, and related records
  • AI-powered actions such as converting estimates, checking project status, and generating timelines
  • Automations that could handle operational tasks without relying on people to do everything manually

This is the type of build that separates a basic AI website generator from a serious business app builder. A real tool has to look good, yes, but it also needs to be scalable, usable, safe, and connected to the work happening behind the scenes.

How Each Tool Handles the Initial Build

At the very beginning, these platforms appear fairly similar. You enter a prompt, describe the app you want, and let AI start putting the pieces together. But the differences become obvious almost immediately.

Base44: Fast to Start, But Too Many Assumptions

Base44 simply got to work. It did not ask clarifying questions about the desired workflow, interface, database logic, or the specifics of how the business should operate.

That sounds convenient until you are building something complicated. A contractor management platform has a lot of moving parts. If the AI makes assumptions about how estimates relate to projects, how users access the client portal, or what happens when an invoice is unpaid, those assumptions can create a lot of cleanup later.

The problem is not that Base44 moves fast. The problem is that it can move fast in the wrong direction.

When a platform starts building before it truly understands what you need, you often end up in a long cycle of corrections:

  1. Ask AI to make a change.
  2. Wait for the new version.
  3. Find something else that changed or broke.
  4. Send another prompt.
  5. Use more credits.

For a simple landing page or a lightweight dashboard, that may be fine. For a real client-facing tool, it can get frustrating and expensive quickly.

Lovable: Better Planning Before It Builds

Lovable takes a more considered approach. It asks questions, creates a plan, and gives you a chance to approve what it intends to build before jumping into development.

That is a definite improvement over blindly generating an app. Being able to review the plan helps reduce some of the guesswork and gives you more visibility into the proposed structure.

Lovable is a solid option for building websites, apps, and tools. It also supports turning tools into MCP servers so AI systems such as ChatGPT and Claude can interact with them. That is an interesting capability for people building AI-connected products.

Still, while Lovable plans more carefully than Base44, the biggest issue comes later: making detailed changes can still depend heavily on talking back and forth with AI in chat.

Softr: More Questions Up Front, Less Rework Later

Softr takes the most deliberate route at the planning stage. It asks more in-depth questions, including questions about the app’s appearance, theme, organization, and overall interface.

Some people may see that as extra friction. I see it as the platform trying to get the important stuff right the first time.

When you are building a business application, a few extra questions at the start are a lot cheaper than dozens of correction prompts later. Softr’s approach is built around co-building with AI while still keeping you in control of the outcome.

Pricing Matters When Every Prompt Uses Credits

Vibe coding is not only about what a platform can build. It is also about what it costs to get from the first version to a usable final product.

That is where credit-based pricing becomes important. If every correction requires another AI chat request, then poor control over edits can become a real business cost.

Base44 pricing

Base44 offers a free tier, but it is limited for anyone trying to build something meaningful. The plan positioned for more serious work was shown at around $50 per month, including 250 message credits and 10,000 integration credits.

For full access, the higher plan was shown at approximately $200 per month. That can become costly, especially if the build requires extensive back-and-forth prompting.

Lovable pricing

Lovable follows a similar model. Its business-focused tier is where the more useful features become available, but it includes only 100 credits per month. Reaching the 250-credit range comparable to Base44 can place monthly spending somewhere between $100 and $200.

Again, that is not necessarily a problem if the app is right quickly. But when edits, fixes, and refinements all require AI chat, the credits can disappear fast.

Softr pricing

Softr’s plans were presented with tiers around $59, $169, and $323 per month. The important part is what the entry paid plan includes: substantial workflow actions, database actions, and AI credits.

At the approximately $59 tier, you are not stripped of the key capabilities needed for a serious operational app. That makes a big difference for builders who need databases, workflows, internal tools, client portals, and automation without immediately being pushed into a high-cost plan.

The main cost lesson: the sticker price matters, but the cost of rework matters more. A platform that lets you edit visually can save a huge amount over the life of a project because you are not spending credits every time you want to move, change, hide, or configure something.

What Happened When the Contractor Apps Were Finished

All three platforms produced an interface that initially looked promising. The real test was whether the application could actually function as a usable business system.

Base44 created an appealing but limited result

The Base44 build produced a visually attractive dashboard with clients, estimates, projects, invoicing, and an AI assistant. The assistant included actions such as estimate conversion, project status checks, and timeline generation.

On the surface, it looked good. But the underlying database structure behaved more like placeholder data than a fully functional operational backend. The database existed, but it was not meaningfully connected to real actions in the app.

Base44 had also introduced workflows, but access required an upgrade to a larger plan. That makes it harder to evaluate and use the automation capability without increasing spend.

Lovable struggled with functionality

Lovable also generated the basic app components, including database-related areas and an authentication flow. But the functional experience did not hold up in the test.

The signup process was slow and failed to properly create an account. Attempts to access the dashboard returned to the authentication screen instead of allowing entry into the app.

This is the difference between an attractive generated concept and a tool that is ready for use. If authentication breaks, the rest of the app does not matter much.

Softr produced a functional business application

Softr delivered the most complete result. The generated app included a dashboard, projects, estimates, invoices, a project calendar, and navigable sections for the business workflow.

More importantly, it provided control over the application after the initial AI-generated build.

For example, if you need a new area for people to upload custom CSV files or PDFs, you can add a block and ask AI to create the custom functionality. Softr can generate that block even if there is no prebuilt preset for it.

Once the block exists, you can move it, change it, expand it, and configure it visually. You are not locked into hoping an AI chat response gets every detail right.

Softr created a custom database for the contractor build, including users, clients, estimates, and projects. That is the foundation required to make the interface useful instead of decorative.

Then there are workflows. Softr allows you to create custom automated flows using triggers, schedules, built-in actions, and integrations.

Consider a simple contractor payment example. You could create a scheduled workflow that identifies unpaid invoices and sends a reminder email. That is a small automation, but it removes a repetitive manual task and helps ensure that payment follow-up does not get forgotten.

That same workflow approach can be applied across a business:

  • Automate internal tasks that currently depend on manual handoffs.

This is why Softr stands out as more than a replacement for a basic website builder. It combines the interface, database, workflows, and integrations in one environment instead of forcing you to duct tape multiple tools together.
