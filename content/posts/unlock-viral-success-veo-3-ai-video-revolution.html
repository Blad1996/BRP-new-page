🎬 Crafting Viral AI Videos: The Power of Humor and Provocative Hooks

The essence of viral AI videos lies in their ability to grab attention instantly. One of the most effective ways to do this is through humor combined with provocative or unexpected visuals. For example, a video showing Jesus on the cross smiling and whispering "God is about to BRB" creates an unhinged, attention-grabbing hook that stops viewers from scrolling.

Humor in these videos often plays on blending iconic, serious biblical stories or historical scenes with modern slang, influencer culture, or absurd twists. For instance:

  • Samson taking pre-workout and saying, "These Philistines thought they could flex on me. Big mistake."
  • David preparing to "yeet" a stone at Goliath.
  • Noah rapping "I got the horses in the back" from Old Town Road while boarding the ark.
  • Moses parting the Red Sea and telling people to stop "stacking bricks in Egypt."

This style of humor is about establishing a serious norm and then breaking it with an unexpected twist, similar to the comedic structure used by SNL or Key and Peele. The comedic tension arises from the contrast between the reverence of the original story and the irreverent, modern reinterpretation.

Interestingly, this approach flirts with the line of being provocative without being blasphemous, appealing to a broad audience that spans from those familiar with the stories to those who enjoy edgy humor.

🧠 The Creative Process: From Idea to Viral Video

Creating viral AI videos is a blend of creativity, scripting, and prompt engineering. The process typically involves:

  1. Identifying a strong hook: This could be a provocative visual or a humorous twist on a well-known story that stops viewers from scrolling.
  2. Writing a shot list: Breaking down the video into key scenes or moments that convey the story or joke.
  3. Using AI to generate prompts: Feeding the shot list into ChatGPT or similar tools to create detailed video prompts formatted for VEO 3.
  4. Iterating and refining: Running prompts through VEO 3, reviewing outputs, and adjusting prompts to improve character consistency, scene accuracy, and comedic timing.
  5. Final editing and publishing: Selecting the best clips, assembling the video, and posting on platforms like TikTok or X (formerly Twitter) for maximum reach.

For example, an idea like “Jesus waiting in the tomb with no WiFi or snacks” translates into a humorous prompt where Jesus is bored and whispering secrets, creating a relatable and funny modern angle on an ancient story.

Prompt engineering is crucial. Effective prompts include a description of the scene, the dialogue, character performance notes, and environmental details such as lighting and time of day. Using quotation marks around dialogue can also influence subtitle generation, although sometimes subtitles may appear as gibberish.
